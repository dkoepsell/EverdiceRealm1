# What's new in Everdice: announcement copy

Drafts for the release on the `table-night` branch. Both link to the public page at
https://realmofeverdice.com/whats-new, which shows the same cards as the in-app popup.

## Discord post

**Have you played Everdice lately?**

If your last visit felt like a lot of talking and not much trouble, that's on us, and we've fixed it. Here's what's new:

⚔️ **More danger, and you set the dial.** Ambushes, traps and rivals now arrive on a rhythm. Pick Story, Balanced or Action for each campaign.
💀 **Smarter monsters, real battlefields.** Wolves flank, goblins hide and shoot, and every fight happens somewhere with cover, high ground and things to knock over.
⚡ **Instant combat rounds** for the fights that are a formality. Real rolls, one tap.
🧝 **Companions with something to say.** They approve, object, joke at camp, roll their own skills, and might walk out if you push them too far.
🧭 **Alignment as a compass, not a cage.** The world reacts to who you are, and your Journal shows what your deeds actually say about you.
📜 **Backgrounds open doors.** Acolytes find shelter at temples, criminals have contacts, sages know where to look. No roll needed.
🎲 **One-shot nights.** Send a link, and friends join in a minute with a ready-made level 3 hero. No account needed to start.
🤝 **Friends can play your companions.** Hand your cleric to a friend and let them decide what she does.

Simply put, Everdice is now a game you can bring people to. Try it this weekend: pick a night, hit **Invite friends** on your campaign, and drop the link here.

Everything's listed at https://realmofeverdice.com/whats-new

## Email

**Subject:** Have you played Everdice lately?

**Preview text:** More danger, livelier companions, and a way to bring your friends.

Hi {first_name},

It's been a little while since you were at the table, so I wanted to tell you what we've been building, because a lot has changed.

The biggest complaint we heard was that adventures could drift into long conversations with very little at stake. That's fair. Real D&D has rhythm: you talk, you scheme, and then something goes wrong and you have to roll for it. So we've taught the game to push back. Ambushes, traps, rivals and hard choices now arrive on a steady beat, and you choose how hard each campaign pushes (Story, Balanced or Action). Monsters fight the way the rules intend, too. Wolves flank, goblins hide and shoot, and every fight happens somewhere, with cover, high ground and hazards you can turn against them. When a fight is a formality, you can resolve the whole round in one tap.

Your companions were too quiet, so they have opinions now. They approve, object, warn you, joke at camp, roll their own skills and get scenes of their own. Alignment works the way it does at a good table: the world notices who you are, but nothing stops you from doing what you choose, and your Journal shows what your deeds actually say about you. Backgrounds come with their D&D features, so an acolyte can find shelter at a temple and a criminal can call on a contact, no roll needed.

The part I'm most excited about is this: you can bring friends. Send an invite link and they can join in a minute with a ready-made level 3 hero, no account needed to start. You can even hand one of your companions to a friend and let them decide what that character does. In other words, Everdice now works for a one-shot night, not just a solo campaign.

A few ways to try it:

- Set a campaign to Action and see how long you last.
- Ask a companion what they really think of you.
- Play against your alignment for a few scenes, then check your Journal.
- Pick a night, invite two friends, and give one of them your cleric.

Everything new is at https://realmofeverdice.com/whats-new

See you at the table,
David
