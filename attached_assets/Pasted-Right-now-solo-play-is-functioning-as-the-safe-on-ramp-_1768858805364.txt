Right now, solo play is functioning as the “safe on-ramp” into Everdice. Users are testing the waters, learning the interface, and building confidence without social risk. The goal is not to disrupt that, but to gently widen the funnel outward.

Below is a steering strategy that respects how they already behave while nudging them toward (1) learning D&D, (2) trying other modes, and (3) coming back.

1. Treat Solo Adventure as the Canonical Onboarding Path (Not a Dead End)

First, lean into what they are already doing.

Reframe solo play explicitly as:

“Solo Adventure (Learn D&D at Your Own Pace)”

Then, instrument it as a tutorial that naturally points elsewhere.

Add “What You’re Actually Learning” Moments

After certain solo milestones, surface a short, non-intrusive panel:

You just used a core D&D mechanic

• Ability checks
• Narrative choice consequences
• DM-guided outcomes

👉 These are the same tools used in group games

CTA:

“See how this works in a party”

“Try this with friends”

This turns solo play into explicit skill acquisition.

2. Soft Mode Switching Inside the Solo Adventure

Instead of asking users to leave solo mode, let solo mode mutate.

A. “Ghost Party” Upgrade (Very Low Friction)

After a few solo sessions:

Want help?

Add AI companions to your adventure:
• Fighter
• Rogue
• Cleric

This introduces:

Party dynamics

Turn structure

Shared narrative

Without:

Scheduling

Social anxiety

Rules overload

Later, you can say:

“These companions can be replaced with real players.”

B. “Drop-In Co-Op” Prompt

If someone returns more than once:

Play this scene with others

Let others join just for the next encounter.

Key point:
Do not pitch a campaign. Pitch one scene.

3. Micro-Learning Instead of D&D Rule Dumps

Avoid “Learn D&D” pages. Nobody clicks those.

Instead, embed 30–60 second knowledge bites tied to actions.

Examples

After a roll:

“Why did this succeed?”
Ability score + modifier + difficulty

After a choice:

“D&D is a game of consequences, not right answers.”

After combat:

“In group play, players coordinate turns like this…”

Each ends with:

“Want to see this live?”

4. Explicit Pathways on the Home Screen (But Not Pushy)

Add three clearly labeled paths, visually equal, emotionally different:

🧭 Play Solo

Learn D&D. No pressure. Just story.

🎲 Play Together

Co-op adventures with friends or strangers.

🧙 Learn to Run Games

Try being the Dungeon Master.

Important:
Do not hide DMing behind expertise language. Frame it as experimentation.

5. Convert Return Visits Into Exploration

Right now, returning users default to solo again. Intercept that once, gently.

On Second or Third Visit:

Welcome back

Last time you played solo.

Want to try something new today?

Buttons:

Continue Solo

Add AI Party

Join a One-Shot

Watch a Game in Progress (spectator mode later)

Even if 90% click “Continue Solo”, the seed is planted.

6. Lightweight Social Proof (Without Testimonials Yet)

With only 19 users, don’t fake scale.

Instead use honest framing:

“Most players start solo.”

“Many try co-op on their second or third visit.”

This normalizes progression.

7. Repo-Level Hooks (for EverdiceRealm1)

In the GitHub repo, reinforce this philosophy so contributors align with it:

README Additions

Add a section like:

Design Philosophy: Gentle Onboarding

Everdice assumes most users:
• are new to D&D
• feel social or rules anxiety
• want to try before committing

Solo play is the default entry point,
not the final destination.

Future Feature Flags

Document stubs like:

This makes your intent legible to future collaborators.
