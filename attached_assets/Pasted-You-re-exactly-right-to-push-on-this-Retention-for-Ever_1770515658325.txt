# 1. How to Improve Session 1 (Design Guide)

## The core shift

Session 1 must answer this question **for the player**:

> “What kind of person is my character becoming?”

Not:

* “What happened in the story?”
* “What system is this?”
* “What’s the world like?”

Those are secondary.

---

## A. Session 1 is about *trajectory*, not mastery

You do **not** want players to:

* level up dramatically
* unlock complex abilities
* optimize builds

You want them to:

* *notice themselves changing*
* *sense direction*
* *see a difference between before and after*

### Practical rules

In Session 1:

* At least **one skill improves slightly**
* At least **one habit is formed**
* At least **one tool is used better the second time than the first**
* At least **one choice reveals something about how they act under uncertainty**

This can be subtle. Subtle is good.

---

## B. Make growth *interpretive*, not numeric (at first)

Early growth should feel like:

* “I’m getting better at this”
* “I handled that differently than before”
* “I’m learning how to approach problems”

Not:

* “+2 stealth”
* “Unlocked perk”

Those come later.

### Examples

* First lockpick attempt: clumsy, slow, uncertain
* Second attempt (same session): calmer, more deliberate
* The text reflects *improvement*, even if the mechanic is identical

Narrative acknowledgment of growth is *huge* for retention.

---

## C. Tools must visibly improve through use

This is one of your strongest levers.

In Session 1:

* Give the character **one simple tool**
* Force them to use it imperfectly once
* Let them use it *better* later

Example tools:

* Lantern
* Rope
* Map
* Charm
* Blade
* Spell focus

The key is that the **tool feels dumb at first** and *smarter later*, even if the system is faking it.

---

## D. Teach the player how Everdice wants to be played

Session 1 should quietly teach:

* patience is rewarded
* observation matters
* preparation changes outcomes
* rash action leaves residue

Do this *through outcomes*, not tutorial text.

---

## E. End Session 1 before resolution, after identity formation

The worst thing you could do is let Session 1 “wrap up nicely.”

The best thing is to let it end at the moment where:

> “Oh. I see who I might become.”

And then stop.

# 2. Session 1 Retention Contract (Non-Negotiable)

This is the internal design contract you enforce on yourself and the system.

### Contract name

**“Session 1 Must Create Obligation”**

### The contract rules

#### Rule 1: The character must change in a describable way

By the end of Session 1, the player should be able to say:

* “My character is becoming more cautious / perceptive / resolute / conflicted / skilled at X.”

If you cannot summarize the change in one sentence, the session failed.

---

#### Rule 2: One skill must show narrative improvement

Even if the mechanics don’t change yet, the narration must clearly signal:

* improvement
* learning
* adaptation

This creates future-oriented motivation.

---

#### Rule 3: One tool must feel more competent by the end

The same tool must:

* feel awkward or insufficient early
* feel more effective later

This teaches investment and mastery.

---

#### Rule 4: One choice must create deferred consequences

Not resolved.
Not explained.
Named, but postponed.

This is the hook.

---

#### Rule 5: Session 1 must end with an explicit *return promise*

The system must say, in-world or near-world:

> “This is not finished.”

Never imply it. Always state it.

# 3. Mandatory First-Session Ending Scene (Drop-In Template)

This is a **required scene** that always triggers at the end of Session 1, regardless of branch.

It adapts its flavor, but not its function.

---

## Scene Title (internal)

**The Quiet Reckoning**

---

## Trigger Conditions

* Session length reached (or)
* First meaningful choice arc completed (or)
* Player attempts to continue beyond designed Session 1 scope

---

## Scene Structure

### 1. Acknowledge the character’s growth

This must reference:

* how they acted
* what they learned
* what improved

Example (adaptive text):

> You pause, if only for a moment.
>
> Earlier, you would have rushed this. Now, you hesitate.
> You’ve learned how the light falls here.
> You’ve learned when to move, and when not to.
>
> You’re not the same as when you began.

This is *crucial*. This makes the character feel worth returning to.

---

### 2. Acknowledge a tool’s improved use

Example:

> You adjust your grip on the lantern.
> Before, it was just something you carried.
> Now, it’s something you use.

This subtly signals mastery progression.

---

### 3. Name the unresolved consequence

This must be explicit and concrete, but unresolved.

Examples:

* “Someone has noticed the path you took.”
* “A decision you made has shifted something nearby.”
* “What you avoided will not remain avoided.”
* “This will surface again.”

No explanation yet.

---

### 4. Freeze the moment

End *before* action.

Example:

> You have the sense that the next step will matter more than the last.
>
> That is where things pause.

This creates narrative tension without cliffhanger melodrama.

---

### 5. Return promise (out-of-world, but soft)

This can be UI-adjacent text:

> **This choice is still unfolding.**
>
> You can return to see what it becomes.

This legitimizes stopping while encouraging return.

---

## Why this works

* The player leaves feeling **formed, not stalled**
* The character feels **invested-with**, not disposable
* The world feels **responsive but patient**
* Returning feels like **continuing responsibility**, not replaying content

---

## One last blunt truth

People return for:

* characters they care about becoming
* tools they are learning to use
* consequences that haven’t finished speaking

This design gives you all three **in Session 1**, without betraying Everdice’s quiet soul.
