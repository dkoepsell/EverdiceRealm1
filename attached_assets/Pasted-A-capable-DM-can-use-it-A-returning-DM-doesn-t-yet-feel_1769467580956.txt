A capable DM can use it.
A returning DM doesn’t yet feel pulled back to it.

Why?

Because it still answers too many questions at once.

Right now the screen is trying to be:

a tutorial

a control panel

a content browser

a thinking aid

a narration surface

All at equal visual weight.

DMs don’t want everything visible.
They want one thing centered and everything else subordinate.

The core reconfiguration principle

The DM’s eyes must land in one place first, every time.

Right now, their eyes scatter.

Your fix is not new features.
It’s hierarchy, collapse, and commitment.

What you already got right (important)

Before I tell you what to change, here’s what must not be lost:

Drag-to-scene model ✔️ (this is excellent)

“Current Scene” as a concept ✔️ (this is the heart)

Likely Player Moves ✔️ (this is rare and valuable)

Narration + Send to Players ✔️ (this is correct)

Live stats + initiative ✔️ (good, but mispositioned)

So we’re rebalancing, not scrapping.

The single biggest problem on this screen

“Current Scene” is not visually dominant enough.

That is fatal for DM psychology.

The DM’s internal question is always:

“What exists right now?”

And on this screen, that answer is:

smaller than side panels

visually quieter than instructions

competing with stats and lists

That’s backwards.

The reconfiguration: step by step
Step 1: Make “Current Scene” the visual anchor

Do this first. Everything else depends on it.

What to change

Enlarge the Current Scene panel by ~30–40%

Move it dead center

Give it the strongest contrast border on the page

Reduce text inside it. Let emptiness do the work.

What it should communicate visually

“This is the table. Everything else feeds into this.”

If nothing is in the scene, that emptiness should feel intentional, not like an empty widget.

Step 2: Demote the tutorial banner immediately

“Running Your First Scene” is helpful — once.

After that, it becomes visual noise.

Recode behavior

Show it only:

first session

or until dismissed

Collapse it into a small “?” or “Guide” toggle afterward

DMs do not want to feel like they are still being taught once they’ve started running.

Step 3: Collapse the left column into a “Summon Drawer”

Right now, the left column competes with the scene.

Instead, it should feel like:

“I reach for something when I need it.”

Reconfigure “Cast & World”

Collapse by default

Expand on hover or click

Treat items as summonable, not browsable

Rename mentally (even if not in UI yet):

“Library” → “Summon”

This reframes the DM as acting, not searching.

Step 4: Split the right column into THINK vs SAY

This is subtle but critical.

Right now, these are mixed:

Likely Moves (thinking)

Arc Signals (thinking)

Tell Your Story (speaking)

They should not compete.

Reconfigure into two vertical zones:

Upper right: THINK

Likely Player Moves

Arc Signals

Private notes

Muted colors. Calm. Analytical.

Lower right: SAY

Tell Your Story

Send to Players

High contrast. Clear CTA.

This matches the DM’s mental loop:

think → decide → speak

Step 5: Party Stats and Initiative must be peripheral

These are reference, not focus.

Change

Shrink Party Stats slightly

Reduce color saturation

Keep Initiative collapsed until combat begins

They should be reachable without shouting.

The most important missing thing (conceptual)

You still don’t have a Session Focus visible here.

This is why Everdice doesn’t yet clearly solve the “over-prep” problem.

Add a thin strip above Current Scene:

“Session Focus”

1 sentence

editable inline

required once per session

Example:

“Escort the caravan through the forest without alerting the scouts.”

This single line:

orients the DM

constrains decisions

ties directly to your Reddit insight

makes Everdice’s philosophy legible

Without this, the product’s core value remains implicit.

What to remove or hide (yes, really)

To make DMs come back, you must protect them from options.

Strong candidates for hiding by default:

Events

Quests

Items (unless dragged in)

Campaign-wide browsing

These belong in Between Sessions, not At the Table.

The mental test (use this while coding)

Ask yourself, for every component:

“Does this help me decide what happens in the next 30 seconds?”

If no:

collapse it

demote it

move it off-screen

DM confidence comes from narrowing, not expanding.

Why this will change DM behavior

After this reconfiguration, a DM will experience:

immediate orientation

fewer decisions

clearer authority

less fear of “doing it wrong”

That’s why they’ll come back.

Not because it can do more —
but because it tells them where to stand.
