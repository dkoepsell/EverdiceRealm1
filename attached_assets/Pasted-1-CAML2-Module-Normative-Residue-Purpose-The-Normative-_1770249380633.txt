1. CAML2 Module: Normative Residue
Purpose

The Normative Residue module formalizes irreversible or partially irreversible consequences that persist across scenes, processes, and sessions, constraining future play.

It distinguishes:

Temporary state (damage, conditions, alerts)

From enduring relational damage (lost standing, foreclosed trust, institutional debt)

This module answers one question:

What remains true even after the scene is over?

Core Design Principles

Residue is not binary (not just “on/off”)

Residue is not automatically repairable

Residue constrains future options, not just narration

Residue always has a bearer (NPC, faction, place, institution, party)

Module Structure
A. Residue Declaration
normative_residue:
  catalog:
    - id: RES_TRUST_ELORIA
      bearer: NPC_Archmage
      domain: social
      description: >
        Loss of Archmage Eloria’s trust due to perceived recklessness.
      severity: 2
      max_severity: 3
      enduring: true
      visible_to_players: true


Required fields

bearer: who carries the residue

domain: social | institutional | metaphysical | political | arcane

severity: current level

enduring: whether it persists across sessions

B. Residue Triggers

Residue is created by attributable failures, not random events.

residue_triggers:
  - id: RT_LIBRARY_DESECRATION
    caused_by: PROC_Library_Puzzle
    condition: failure
    produces:
      residue: RES_TRUST_ELORIA
      delta: +1
      reason: "Sacred tomes mishandled"


Key constraint:

Triggers must be tied to player choice, delay, or refusal

C. Residue Effects

Residue must do something mechanically.

residue_effects:
  - residue: RES_TRUST_ELORIA
    at_severity: 1
    effects:
      - revoke_role: Advisor
  - residue: RES_TRUST_ELORIA
    at_severity: 2
    effects:
      - lock_transition: CONN_SANCTUM_GUIDANCE
  - residue: RES_TRUST_ELORIA
    at_severity: 3
    effects:
      - flip_attitude: hostile
      - enable_process: PROC_ELORIA_WITHDRAWS


If residue does not:

revoke

lock

alter access

change roles

…it is not real residue.

D. Repair Pathways (Optional, Costly)

Repair is not guaranteed.

repair_options:
  - id: REPAIR_RECONCILE_ELORIA
    targets: [RES_TRUST_ELORIA]
    cost:
      time: 1_session
      sacrifice: ITEM_RARE
    success_conditions:
      requires_severity_at_most: 2
    outcomes:
      on_success:
        delta: -1
      on_failure:
        delta: +1


Important:

Repair can fail

Repair can worsen residue

Repair can be refused by NPCs or institutions

E. Non-Reset Constraint

Explicitly block resets.

constraints:
  - residue: RES_TRUST_ELORIA
    cannot_be_removed_by:
      - long_rest
      - spell
      - explanation
      - time_passage


This is what distinguishes normative residue from damage.

Minimal Compliance Rule (for validators)

Every CAML2 adventure must include at least:

One residue that can reach an unrecoverable state

One process that can generate it

One future option that it forecloses

2. One-Page DM Rule
Some Things Cannot Be Fixed

A rule for running games where actions matter.

What This Rule Is

This rule reminds the DM of one simple truth:

Not every problem exists to be solved.
Some exist to be lived with.

Players do not need harsher consequences.
They need lasting ones.

What “Cannot Be Fixed” Means

“Cannot be fixed” does not mean:

The game is unwinnable

The players are punished

Hope is removed

It means:

The world remembers

Repair has limits

Loss changes what comes next

When to Apply This Rule

Apply this rule whenever:

Players ignore warnings

Players choose speed over care

Players exploit safety

Players walk away from responsibility

Players say “we’ll deal with that later”

That later is where residue lives.

What Should Become Unfixable

You do not need many irreversible consequences.
One or two per campaign is enough.

Good candidates:

Trust from a powerful NPC

Access to a unique place

Moral authority in a community

Stability of an institution

A sacred or dangerous object

Never make:

Basic survival unfixable

Core campaign progress impossible

Player dignity contingent on success

How to Signal It (Without Explaining)

You don’t announce irreversibility.

You show it:

The door does not reopen

The letter goes unanswered

The ritual no longer works

The NPC refuses to meet your eyes

If players ask “Can we fix this?”
Answer honestly.

Sometimes the answer is simply:

“No. That time has passed.”

Repair Still Exists (But It Is Not Guaranteed)

If repair is possible:

It should cost time, power, or opportunity

It should compete with other goals

It should never fully erase the past

If repair is refused:

Let that refusal stand

Do not substitute a different fix

Do not soften it later

Why This Makes the Game Better

When some things cannot be fixed:

Choices become real

Success feels earned

Failure feels meaningful

Players stop gaming the system

The story belongs to them, not the plot

Most importantly:

Players stop asking “What happens if I click this?”
and start asking “Can we live with this?”

That is the moment a campaign becomes memorable.
