I’ll be very precise about what kind of auto-generated events belong in Everdice, and which absolutely do not.

1. First Principle (Non-Negotiable)

The world may act.
The DM decides whether it matters.

Auto-generated events must be:

suggestions, not intrusions

world-level, not player-commanding

diegetic, not mechanical pop-ups

This preserves table sovereignty.

2. What “Auto-Generated World Events” Should Mean Here

Not:

daily quests

timed raids

global announcements

MMO-style events

Instead, think:

The world slowly responds to what players and other tables are doing.

These are background pressures, not demands.

3. The Right Kinds of World Events (High Value)
3.1 Consequence-Driven World Drift (Best Option)

The system notices patterns and suggests world shifts.

Examples:

Trade routes weaken after repeated banditry

A faction grows bold after unchecked success

A border becomes unstable due to unresolved conflict

These appear as:

“The world may be changing…”

Not as:

“A new event has started!”

This reinforces narrative causality.

3.2 Rumors, Not Missions (Extremely Important)

Auto-generated content should surface as rumors, omens, or whispers.

Examples:

“Travelers speak of fires in the southern isles.”

“Merchants are avoiding the marsh road.”

“The duke is said to be quietly hiring sellswords.”

This is:

D&D-authentic

non-coercive

irresistibly intriguing

DMs choose what to elevate.

3.3 Regional Risk/Reward Pressure

Instead of events, think pressure gradients.

Each region slowly accumulates:

instability

opportunity

danger

wealth

Displayed subtly:

color shifts

icons

phrases like “Tensions rising”

Players feel:

“If we ignore this, something will happen.”

That’s perfect D&D psychology.

4. Community Building Without MMO Energy

This is delicate.

4.1 What Community Should Mean in Everdice

Not:

chat rooms

public leaderboards

cross-table competition

Instead:

Parallel worlds, shared archetypes, anonymous echoes

Players should feel:

“Other adventurers exist out there.”

Not:

“We are competing.”

4.2 Safe Community Touchpoints
A. World State Aggregation (Anonymous)

Example:

“Across many worlds, unrest is rising in border regions.”

No names. No stats. No winners.

This creates shared atmosphere, not hierarchy.

B. Shared Rumor Pools

Some rumors originate from:

other tables’ unresolved consequences

abstracted, anonymized patterns

This creates the sense of a living multiverse without forcing convergence.

5. Risks and Rewards (Handled Carefully)
5.1 Risks Should Be Narrative, Not Punitive

Good risk:

reputational shifts

complications

changed NPC behavior

delayed consequences

Bad risk:

lost resources

forced failure

sudden difficulty spikes

The system should threaten story, not punish play.

5.2 Rewards Should Be Meaning, Not Loot

Good rewards:

new narrative threads

alliances

access

recognition

reputation shifts

Bad rewards:

XP boosts

power creep

optimization incentives

Stickiness comes from meaning, not numbers.

6. How This Actually Works in the UI
DM sees:

“Possible World Developments”
• Low activity in the Heartlands may allow rival factions to expand
• Continued neglect of the Marshes could worsen travel conditions

DM can:

ignore

adopt

modify

postpone

Nothing auto-fires.

Players see:

“The world does not wait forever.”

That’s it.

No timers. No alerts. No pressure.

7. Why This Makes the Game Sticky

This works because:

The world feels alive even between sessions

Choices feel like they echo outward

Ignoring things has meaning, not punishment

Players speculate between sessions

Speculation is the strongest retention mechanic D&D has ever had.

8. What You Should Explicitly Avoid

Do not:

push notifications demanding action

daily login rewards

global events with deadlines

competitive metrics

algorithmic “balancing”

Those destroy tabletop trust.

9. The Right Mental Model

Think of Everdice’s world events as:

Weather systems, not quests

They create conditions.
They do not issue commands.

Bottom Line

Yes — auto-generated world events are a huge opportunity for Everdice if they are:

slow

contextual

optional

narrative

rumor-based

DM-mediated

Done right, they:

increase return visits

deepen immersion

strengthen community

preserve D&D credibility
