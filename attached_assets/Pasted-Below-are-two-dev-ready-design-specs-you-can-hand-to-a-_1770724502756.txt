Below are **two dev-ready design specs** you can hand to a team. They assume your current Everdice foundations: hex map UI, scene generation pipeline, character persistence, combat handling, CAML assets (or CAML-like), and your emphasis on low-pressure onboarding.

I wrote these as if they are going straight into an internal product doc.

# SPEC 1: Free-Explore Mode

## 1. Overview

**Feature name:** Free-Explore (internally), “Wander” (UI label)
**Purpose:** A low-pressure loop where players move across the world map, discover points of interest, trigger light narrative moments, and occasionally face danger, with meaningful rewards and breadcrumbs into deeper content.

**Primary outcomes**

* Make the map feel alive and playable without committing to a full narrative session.
* Increase session frequency by enabling 2–10 minute “micro-play.”
* Generate durable world knowledge: landmarks, rumors, faction signals, keys, and hooks that later connect to story arcs and dungeon crawls.

**Non-goals**

* Not a full campaign replacement.
* Not a loot grind.
* Not “random fights per tile.”

## 2. Player Experience

### Entry points

* From map screen: button “Wander”
* From Lantern Hall: “Take a walk outside the walls”
* From character page: “Go exploring”

### Core loop (2–10 min)

1. Player selects a character party (solo or party) and clicks a neighboring hex.
2. Move resolves.
3. The system generates one of:

   * Discovery: landmark, trail, rumor, resource, NPC trace
   * Quiet event: weather, strange sound, minor choice
   * Risk event: hazard, ambush, pursuit
   * Nothing notable (rare, and never consecutive more than once)
4. Player chooses to:

   * Continue wandering
   * Investigate further (spawns a micro-scene)
   * Return to safety (Lantern Hall or last safe node)
5. Outcomes produce: map annotations, journal entries, minor loot, or hooks.

### Key UX principles

* Low latency. Post-click response should begin immediately with a short “reveal” line, then deep detail can stream in.
* Quiet tone. Avoid heavy exposition unless player opts into “Investigate.”
* Always offer a safe exit. “Head back” should never punish.
* Information is a reward. Players should feel they learned something even without combat.

## 3. World Model and Content Types

### Hex states

Each hex can be:

* **Unknown** (fog of war)
* **Seen** (visited or revealed)
* **Noted** (player added a note, or system added a discovered marker)
* **Hot** (temporary danger, pursuit, storm front)
* **Depleted** (resource harvested recently)

### Discoverable content categories

Each discovery has:

* id
* name
* category
* short description (one sentence)
* long description (optional, only when investigating)
* tags (faction, biome, danger, mystery, resource)
* persistence behavior (permanent, time-limited, repeatable)

Recommended categories:

* Landmark (ruin, standing stone, shrine, bridge)
* Trace (tracks, campsite, blood trail, broken wagon)
* Resource (herbs, ore, water, salvage)
* NPC Echo (rumor, sign, note, dropped token)
* Hazard (quicksand, unstable ridge, cursed fog)
* Opportunity (merchant camp, lost satchel, friendly guide)

## 4. Mechanics

### Movement

* One move per action.
* Only adjacent hexes by default.
* Optional sprint: move two hexes, increases risk roll and fatigue.

**Constraints**

* Locked hexes may exist (cliffs, borders) and require tools, skills, or keys.

### Risk and Encounter Frequency

Free-Explore must feel alive but not punishing.

**Recommended baseline probabilities (modifiable by biome and player level):**

* Discovery: 45%
* Quiet event: 25%
* Risk event: 20%
* Nothing notable: 10% (never twice in a row)

**Risk event internal split:**

* Hazard: 45%
* Ambush: 35%
* Pursuit: 20%

### Investigation mechanic

After a Discovery or Trace, offer:

* “Mark it and move on”
* “Investigate” (creates a micro-scene card with 1–3 choices)
* “Set camp” (optional, requires supplies, time passes)

Investigation can trigger:

* A small social interaction
* A trap check
* A minor combat
* A clue that unlocks a dungeon crawl or a story scene

### Rewards philosophy

Reward types ranked by frequency:

1. Knowledge and map markers (most common)
2. Consumables and small trinkets
3. Keys, fragments, faction tokens (rare)
4. Equipment upgrades (very rare in Free-Explore)

### Difficulty scaling

Use a “Danger Rating” per hex computed from:

* biome base danger
* proximity to known lairs
* time since last rest
* party level
* hot state

This rating influences risk event chance and encounter strength.

### Resting and time

A minimal time model is enough:

* Each move advances “time ticks”
* Certain events change by time
* Rest consumes ticks, reduces fatigue, lowers danger

You can start with a simple integer tick counter per run.

## 5. Data and Persistence

### Data models

**HexExplorationState**

* userId
* campaignId
* hexId
* discoveredAt
* lastVisitedAt
* state: unknown | seen | noted | hot | depleted
* markers: [MarkerId]
* notes: string (optional)
* dangerOverride: number (optional)
* depletionUntilTick: number (optional)

**Marker**

* id
* campaignId
* hexId
* type: landmark | trace | hazard | resource | npc_echo | opportunity
* title
* blurb
* tags: string[]
* discoveredBy: userId
* createdAt
* persistence: permanent | untilTick | repeatableCooldown
* linkedContent:

  * dungeonId (optional)
  * sceneId (optional)
  * factionId (optional)
  * itemId (optional)

**WanderRun**

* id
* userId
* campaignId
* partyId
* startedAt
* endedAt (nullable)
* currentHexId
* tick
* fatigue
* lastOutcomeType
* flags: json (small)

**WanderOutcomeLog**

* runId
* tick
* fromHexId
* toHexId
* outcomeType
* outcomePayload (json)
* rewardPayload (json)

### Persistence rules

* Seen persists.
* Markers persist based on their persistence field.
* Hot states decay by tick.
* Depletion prevents immediate re-harvest.

## 6. AI and Content Generation

This mode needs controlled generation. Use templates plus AI as a filler, not as the sole authority.

### Content generation pipeline

On move:

1. Determine biome and danger context.
2. Roll outcome category (weighted).
3. If Discovery or Quiet event: select from curated tables where possible.
4. If no curated match: call AI with strict schema.
5. Persist marker or log event.
6. Return a short reveal immediately, stream longer text only if player selects Investigate.

### Required output schema (AI)

**WanderOutcome**

* type: discovery | quiet | risk | none
* title: string (max 60 chars)
* reveal: string (max 180 chars)
* detail: string (max 800 chars, optional)
* choices: array of 1–3 choices

  * id
  * label (max 40 chars)
  * intentTag: investigate | avoid | engage | retreat | camp
  * mechanicalEffects: json (optional)
* rewards: array

  * kind: knowledge | consumable | trinket | key_fragment | faction_token
  * idOrName
  * quantity
* markerToCreate: Marker or null
* combatHook: CombatSeed or null

**CombatSeed**

* encounterType: ambush | pursuit | lair_scout
* enemyTheme: string
* difficulty: easy | medium | hard
* battlefieldTag: woods | road | ruins | swamp
* stakes: string (one sentence)
* fleeAllowed: boolean

### Prompt constraints

* Keep reveal short.
* No long monologues.
* Provide at least one non-combat option whenever possible.
* Rewards must be consistent with rarity rules.

## 7. Combat Integration

When combat triggers:

* Create a standard combat session using existing combat engine.
* Ensure “Flee” is available by default.
* Free-Explore combats should end quickly:

  * fewer enemies
  * simpler abilities
  * clear escape conditions

Combat outcomes should feed back:

* Injury or resource loss
* Loot or clue drops
* “Hot” state added to nearby hexes if pursuit occurs

## 8. UI Requirements

### Map UI

* Toggle: Explore mode indicator “Wandering”
* Hex hover shows:

  * biome
  * danger meter
  * any markers
* Clicking neighbor hex triggers Move and shows a bottom drawer or right panel “Outcome Card”
* Marker icons minimal and consistent.

### Outcome Card

* Title
* Reveal line
* Buttons for 1–3 choices
* Optional: “Mark on map”
* Optional: “Add note”
* Optional: “Head back to Lantern Hall”

### Journal

* “Wander Log” tab with chronological entries
* Markers list and filters

## 9. APIs

Assume REST, adjust if you use websockets.

### Endpoints

* POST /api/wander/start

  * body: { campaignId, partyId, startHexId }
  * returns: WanderRun

* POST /api/wander/move

  * body: { runId, toHexId }
  * returns: WanderOutcome (and maybe streaming token)

* POST /api/wander/choose

  * body: { runId, choiceId }
  * returns: resolution payload (may create marker, start combat, grant reward)

* POST /api/wander/end

  * body: { runId }
  * returns: summary

* GET /api/map/hex/:hexId/state

* GET /api/map/markers?campaignId=...

### Idempotency

* move should be idempotent per tick and target hex.
* choose should be idempotent per outcome.

## 10. Telemetry and Success Metrics

Log:

* time-to-first-outcome after move
* average wander session length
* number of moves per session
* % sessions that include at least one marker
* % sessions that include combat
* return rate within 24h and 7d for wander users

Success targets:

* 30–60% of active users try Wander within first week
* Wander sessions average 4–8 minutes
* At least 65% of Wander sessions produce a marker or journal entry

## 11. Edge Cases

* Player moves repeatedly between two hexes. Use diminishing returns to avoid farming.
* No content available. Guarantee fallback generic events per biome.
* Party death. Offer “rescued back to Lantern Hall” with consequence (lost items, debt, injury).
* Offline refresh mid-run. Run state must be recoverable.

## 12. Rollout Plan

Phase 1: Minimal Wander

* movement + outcomes + markers + simple rewards
* low combat frequency

Phase 2: Investigate micro-scenes

* choice cards that can start mini encounters or clues

Phase 3: Hooks into Dungeon Crawl and campaign scenes

* keys and fragments that unlock delves

# SPEC 2: Dungeon Crawl Mode

## 1. Overview

**Feature name:** Dungeon Crawl (internally), “Delve” (UI label)
**Purpose:** A contained, replayable hex-dungeon experience with navigation, tactical fights, traps, and a culminating reward, designed to feel like tabletop dungeon delving, not a grindy roguelike.

**Primary outcomes**

* Provide a satisfying 10–30 minute play session with clear progression.
* Offer a combat-forward option without making the entire product “one battle after another.”
* Create a strong reward moment: boss, chest, and an unlock that matters outside the dungeon.

**Non-goals**

* Not a full procedural roguelike.
* Not endless scaling difficulty.
* Not purely combat rooms.

## 2. Player Experience

### Entry points

* From a map marker: “Delve the Ruin”
* From Lantern Hall: “Enter a known dungeon” list
* From a discovery hook: “Follow the trail into the earth”

### Core loop (10–30 min)

1. Player selects a dungeon and party.
2. Enters dungeon map (small hex grid).
3. Movement reveals tiles with fog of war.
4. Each tile can host:

   * encounter
   * trap
   * puzzle choice
   * resource cache
   * lore node
   * safe room
5. Player manages resources, chooses routes, can retreat.
6. Reaches boss area.
7. Defeats boss or negotiates, obtains chest reward.
8. Exit and post-run summary and world consequence.

### Tone and philosophy

* Encourage non-combat solutions sometimes.
* Running away is valid.
* The dungeon “remembers” if you leave and return.

## 3. Dungeon Map Model

### Dungeon as a hex micro-map

* Recommended size: 7x7 to 11x11 hexes, depending on desired session length.
* Must include:

  * entrance node
  * boss node
  * 1–3 optional branches
  * at least one shortcut or secret if discovered

### Generation types

Support both:

* **Authored** (preferred for flagship delves, CAML-based)
* **Semi-procedural** (for replayable side delves)

Phase 1 can ship with authored only.

## 4. Mechanics

### Fog of war

* Initially only entrance visible.
* Adjacent hexes revealed on move.
* Some hexes reveal further if “scout” action used.

### Movement and actions

Per turn:

* Move to adjacent hex, or
* Take action in current hex: search, disarm, rest, listen, interact.

### Encounter pacing

Recommended distribution for a 9x9:

* 4–6 encounters
* 2–3 traps
* 1–2 puzzles or social nodes
* 1 boss
* 2 safe or quiet nodes
* 1–2 loot caches

### Combat design constraints

* Standardize encounter length.
* Prefer 2–5 enemies.
* Boss fight can be more involved but must have clear phases.

### Resource pressure

Use simple pressures:

* torches or “light” ticks
* food or “supplies”
* HP and limited consumables
* optional “stress” or “fatigue” counter

### Retreat

* Always possible unless explicitly blocked by boss lock.
* Retreat has cost:

  * dungeon state changes
  * some enemies respawn
  * boss may gain an advantage

## 5. Dungeon Persistence and Memory

The dungeon should feel like a place.

### Persistent elements

* revealed map stays revealed for that party
* cleared encounters remain cleared for a cooldown or until boss defeated
* unlocked doors remain unlocked
* traps disarmed stay disarmed
* puzzles solved stay solved

### Reset logic

After boss defeat:

* dungeon enters “completed” state
* optional weekly reset if you want replayability
* reward table changes and story hooks update

## 6. Data and Persistence

### Data models

**DungeonDefinition**

* id
* name
* themeTags: goblin | undead | ruins | cult
* recommendedLevelRange
* mapLayout:

  * width, height, hex coordinates
  * special nodes list (entrance, boss, chest)
* nodeTable:

  * nodeId
  * coord
  * type: encounter | trap | puzzle | lore | cache | safe | boss | chest
  * contentRef: encounterId | trapId | puzzleId | sceneId
  * onEnterScript: json (optional)
* rewardProfileId
* completionHooks: json (unlock marker, faction change, etc.)

**DungeonRun**

* id
* userId
* campaignId
* dungeonId
* partyId
* startedAt
* endedAt
* currentCoord
* revealedCoords: [coord]
* clearedNodes: [nodeId]
* disarmedTraps: [nodeId]
* solvedPuzzles: [nodeId]
* lightTick
* supplies
* flags: json

**DungeonNodeState**

* runId
* nodeId
* state: hidden | revealed | cleared | failed | locked
* lastResolvedAt
* resolutionPayload: json

**DungeonReward**

* runId
* itemDrops
* knowledgeDrops
* unlockDrops
* goldValue (if you track gold)
* grantedAt

## 7. Encounter, Trap, Puzzle Content Specs

### EncounterDefinition

* id
* theme
* difficulty
* enemyRoster (with counts)
* battlefieldTag
* tacticsHint
* alternateResolutions:

  * bribe
  * intimidate
  * sneak
  * negotiate
* lootProfileId
* clueDrops (optional)

### TrapDefinition

* id
* trigger
* detectDC
* disarmDC
* consequences
* partialSuccess outcomes

### PuzzleDefinition

Keep puzzles simple and choice-based at first:

* id
* setupText
* options (2–4)
* correctOutcome
* failOutcome
* hintMechanic (spend time, spend resource, skill check)

## 8. AI and Authoring

For Dungeon Crawl you want higher determinism.

### Two-tier content strategy

* Tier A: authored content for nodes (best)
* Tier B: AI fills text wrappers and variation while respecting node schema

AI should not invent new mechanics mid-run.

### AI output schema for node narration

**DungeonNodeNarration**

* title (max 60)
* description (max 500)
* options (array)

  * id
  * label (max 40)
  * resolutionType: combat | check | choice | loot | lore
  * dc (optional)
  * successText (optional)
  * failText (optional)

Use AI mainly for tone and flavor, not rules.

## 9. Combat Integration

### Entry

When player enters an encounter node:

* lock movement until resolved or retreat option chosen
* spawn combat session with node’s enemy roster and battlefieldTag

### Flee behavior

* always present unless boss node
* fleeing moves party back one node and increases lightTick
* may mark node as “alerted” raising difficulty next time

### Boss node

Boss should have:

* phase triggers based on HP or rounds
* at least one non-combat alternative if discovered (secret, bargain, hostage, relic)

## 10. Rewards and External Consequences

### Chest design

Chest reward should feel like a moment:

* choose 1 of 3 rewards, or
* take all but trigger a consequence, cursed item, faction hostility

### Reward profile

Include:

* one meaningful item or upgrade
* one knowledge unlock
* one world hook (marker appears, NPC becomes available, new rumor in Lantern Hall)

### External consequences examples

* Unlock “Wander” marker chain in adjacent region
* Unlock a story scene in main campaign
* Increase reputation with faction
* Decrease danger in nearby hexes for a time
* Spawn a new rival or threat meter tick

## 11. UI Requirements

### Dungeon map screen

* Hex micro-map with fog of war
* Node iconography minimal:

  * unknown
  * revealed
  * cleared
  * danger
  * chest
* Side panel:

  * party status
  * resources: light, supplies, HP
  * current node details and actions
* Controls:

  * click-to-move
  * “Search”
  * “Rest”
  * “Retreat”

### Node resolution modal or panel

* short title
* description
* 2–4 buttons for actions
* optional skill check UI
* optional “start combat”

### Post-run summary

* nodes cleared
* loot gained
* consequences unlocked
* “return to map” button that highlights new markers

## 12. APIs

* POST /api/delve/start

  * body: { campaignId, dungeonId, partyId }
  * returns: DungeonRun + initial revealed nodes

* POST /api/delve/move

  * body: { runId, toCoord }
  * returns: node state and narration if any

* POST /api/delve/action

  * body: { runId, actionType, nodeId, optionId? }
  * returns: resolution payload, may start combat

* POST /api/delve/retreat

  * body: { runId }
  * returns: updated run state

* POST /api/delve/end

  * body: { runId }
  * returns: summary + rewards + unlocks

Idempotency required per node resolution.

## 13. Telemetry and Success Metrics

Track:

* start rate from Wander markers vs Lantern Hall
* completion rate
* average duration
* number of retreats
* combat win rate and wipe rate
* chest reward choice distribution
* how often delves unlock further content

Targets:

* 40% of players who start a delve finish it or retreat intentionally
* boss defeat rate around 60–75% depending on difficulty
* at least 50% of completed delves produce a new map marker or story hook

## 14. Edge Cases

* Player disconnect mid-combat: combat session must resume.
* Party wipe: offer “escape with scars” option that costs items and adds a consequence.
* Farming: implement cooldowns or diminishing loot returns for repeat runs.
* Sequence breaking: if boss reached too early, allow but raise difficulty or lock behind key.

## 15. Rollout Plan

Phase 1: Authored “Goblin Warren” delve

* fixed map
* simple encounters
* one boss
* one chest reward choice

Phase 2: Add 2–3 more authored delves with different themes.

Phase 3: Semi-procedural delves from templates.

## Shared Implementation Notes (both features)

### Latency strategy (important for stickiness)

Adopt a 3-step response pattern for every move:

1. **Commit:** confirm move instantly, update map position.
2. **Reveal:** show one-sentence outcome immediately.
3. **Deepen:** stream detail only if player investigates or engages.

This prevents the “post-choice waiting” problem you identified.

### Consistency with DnD feel

To keep these from feeling like a video game:

* Give non-combat options regularly.
* Make information and consequences matter.
* Let retreat be valid.
* Keep the world persistent and reactive.
