What’s Happening Psychologically

When a DM opens this screen, they see:

Controls (Pause, Undo, Inject, Override)

Mode toggles

Queue

AI / Dice

Map

Exploration state

They do not see:

What is unstable in the world

Who wants what

What will explode if ignored

Why tonight matters

So even though your engine is structural and state-driven…

The DM is forced to mentally reconstruct the situation.

That is cognitive load.

And cognitive load kills retention.

The Core Structural Problem

Your UI is organized around:

System Actions

It needs to be organized around:

World Tension

Right now:
Control Panel is primary.
Scene is empty.
Queue is procedural.

Instead, the primary surface should answer instantly:

What is happening right now?

What is about to happen?

What will happen if I do nothing?

What are the players sitting on top of?

What I Would Change (High Impact)
1. Replace “Session Focus” With “World Pressure Overview”

Instead of:

Click to set session focus…

Make this automatic and visible:

🔥 Active Pressures

Cult Ritual – 2 steps from completion

Zyrithan Faction unrest – growing

Valeria suspects betrayal

Weather front approaching ruin

And if there are none?

That’s a problem. Your engine should always have 1–3 active pressures.

DMs want tension to manage.

2. Move Control Panel Down

Pause / Undo / Override is secondary.

It’s meta.

It should not dominate the psychological first impression.

Put it behind a subtle collapsible bar.

Right now it visually competes with the fiction.

3. Transform “Event Queue” Into “Emerging Consequences”

The word “Queue” feels mechanical.

Rename and restructure:

Emerging Consequences

Each item should show:

Trigger source

Why it exists

What it escalates if ignored

Time pressure (if any)

Example:

Investigation: carvings
→ Unlocks hidden faction memory
→ Escalates cult awareness

DM sees impact immediately.

4. Add a “Do Nothing Forecast” Panel

This is where your state-driven engine becomes unbeatable.

Small panel:

If the party stalls…

Cult advances ritual stage

Faction distrust increases

NPC departs town

That alone makes the DM feel like they are managing a living system.

Right now that power is invisible.

5. Current Scene Is Too Empty

The center panel says:

This is your table.

But there’s no tension displayed.

Add:

Scene Stakes

Who is present

Hidden tensions in scene

What could go wrong

You’re thinking structurally already.

Surface it.

The Retention Formula for DMs

A DM comes back when:

They feel like the world runs between sessions

They are surprised in a good way

They look competent

They feel supported, not replaced

Right now your UI feels like:

You are operating a system.

It needs to feel like:

You are conducting a living world.
